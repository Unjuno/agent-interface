# #1890 Probabilistic automaton censoring identifiability result

Decision: **PASS_PROBABILISTIC_AUTOMATON_CENSORING_IDENTIFIABILITY_SCOPED**

For one state-action pair with true next-state probability p=P(A), state-dependent completion-before-censor probabilities a for A and b for B produce observable completed rates x=p*a and y=(1-p)*b.

The completion-only estimate q=x/(x+y) is not generally p. On the frozen exact Fraction grid:

- formal rows: 144
- state-independent a=b rows: 36; q=p in 36/36
- state-dependent a!=b rows: 108; q!=p in 108/108
- bias-sign mismatch: 0; q-p has the sign of a-b
- completion-only naive wrong-p rows: 108
- rows with positive censored mass: 135

Retaining censored count does not by itself identify p when a and b are unknown. The exact observable pair (x,y) formed 132 groups; 12 groups contained more than one distinct true p, covering 24 distinct-p memberships. Every ambiguous p satisfied the analytical feasible interval x <= p <= 1-y.

Concrete witness: observable x=1/10 and y=9/20 is generated by either (p=1/10,a=1,b=1/2) or (p=2/5,a=1/4,b=3/4). The same completed-A/completed-B/censor rates therefore do not identify the underlying transition probability.

Implication for #1658: completion-only transition counts must not be treated as an unbiased online next-state estimator unless the censor/completion mechanism is justified. Censored episodes are first-class evidence. When completion is known state-independent (a=b>0), completion-only normalization is exact in this scoped model.
