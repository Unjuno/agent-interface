## What this narrows

Across these single-run timing points, a tracked delayed second `a` completed and added a second old-task effect at 100 and 250 ms, while at 500 and 8000 ms the owner retired before that effect was observed. The observed transition lies between the tested 250 and 500 ms points on this host/image/schedule; this is not a calibrated deadline or a reliability estimate. The 100 ms direct-injection positive control separately shows that an untracked `b` can remain down at cleanup readback.

The strongest unresolved issue is the 100–250 ms completion race: the caller accepted a cancellation request, but a delayed tracked action could finish afterward. In R06, the execution record showed a verified release while the cancellation/terminal authority remained unknown, and the terminal owner snapshot conflicted with the cancellation retirement claim. Product policy should keep replay denied and not promote UNKNOWN to confirmed based on these observations.

## Limits and next experiment

All effects were synthetic Xvfb window effects. XTEST's injected delay is not a real socket/network delay, application-native behavior, WSLc experiment, MAP01 task, or model-mediated task. The tested task/action schedule is narrow and the runs are single attempts, sequential, not randomized, and host-specific. No speed, token, provider, or general task-success claim is supported.

Next, test the same runtime-tracked second-press condition under the prestarted cancellation supervisor and standard caller with a prospective schedule around 250–500 ms. Independently audit the completion/cancel receipt conflict and make cancellation admission semantics explicit: whether already-admitted input may finish, which receipt owns release truth, and what keeps a fresh attempt fenced.
