# Job-owned stdio under owner death: R594

One fresh Windows no-GUI/no-model trial combined atomic Job membership with child stdio. A worker and its ordinary grandchild both wrote fixed markers to inherited stdout. The owner confirmed their Job membership; the auditor matched independently retained process HANDLEs to their creation times, and duplicated only the owner's stdout read end, not its Job handle or a write end. Before owner termination both processes were active and the reader remained open. After termination, both process HANDLEs signaled and the independent reader reached normal EOF, joined without errors, and closed its stream. No fallback child termination was used.

Conditions, exact sources, markers, result, base64 stdout bytes and empty owner logs are retained in raw.zip. Independent review matched all six source hashes and nonce/identity chain, wait results, EOF, join, stream/handle close and absence of fallback. Read-end identity is supported by the source ownership path; DuplicateHandle return information was not separately journalled. The 0.313-second host observation interval includes setup; equal timestamps after termination do not establish fine-grained termination latency.

This is one small-output sample, not proof of real model communication, bidirectional interruption, all inherited handles, backpressure, concurrent spawn safety, all parent-death boundaries, WSLc recovery, GUI input release, speed or general reliability. Stream/FD ownership transfer interrupted by asynchronous exceptions and closing underneath a blocked reader remain unresolved.
