# Two real guarded owners: old cancellation releases newer same-key hold

One private WSLc/Xvfb allocation used the same local r132 runtime as r142/r146. The first spawned guarded owner pressed `a` and its X sync was blocked with a delayed `b` request queued. The fixture explicitly released the old `a`, independently confirming it was up. A second spawned `OwnedGuardedSessionOwner` on the same display and target then observed, minted and pressed `a` through its own guarded action; its own fixture delayed `b` and blocked sync. Independent keymap confirmed the second `a` down.

The harness called the first owner's normal `cancel_guarded` without wrapping or directly invoking its cleanup. Its receipt reported old child retired, PID-qualified disconnect and release readback verified; independent keymap immediately afterward showed the second owner's `a` up, before the harness cancelled the second owner. The target recorded `a` press/release/press/release, with no `b`, and both tested keys were up at the check after both delayed-request horizons. Both old and second action results were EOF and both close results remained UNKNOWN/no replay; this is not successful task recovery.

This combines real owner-to-owner overlap and the actual normal cancellation cleanup in a single controlled test. The first release between generations and both blocked-sync delays were test injections, so it does not establish spontaneous frequency, a two-MCP-transport scenario, or behavior on a physical desktop. The helper's readback verifies global key state, not ownership attribution. One sample; the private display was destroyed after the run. The risk is relevant to integration of this unmerged candidate on a shared display, and any mitigation needs enforceable display/input custody or another demonstrated isolation boundary.
