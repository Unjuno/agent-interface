# Two guarded owners on one private X display

One fresh private WSLc/Xvfb allocation uses the local r132 candidate and the lightweight trial entry. A real spawned guarded owner sent `a`; the fixture queued a delayed `b` and blocked its X sync, with independent keymap confirming `a` down. Before cancelling that owner, the harness constructed a second `OwnedGuardedSessionOwner` for the same display and target. The second connected, observed, minted a target alias, and executed an admitted `c` key chord. The independent target recorded `a` press, then `c` press/release; independent keymap still showed `a` down and `c` up. The second owner reported its release verified and closed. Thus that receipt does not establish global input neutrality for another owner's `a`.

The harness then cancelled the first owner: the old child retired, its disconnect and `a` release readback were reported, and old cleanup remained UNKNOWN/no replay. At the later check after the delayed-b horizon `a` and `b` were up, and the target recorded the old `a` release with no `b`. Both owners and the isolated display terminated.

This is one direct owner-API composition, with an injected blocked sync and a synthetic target. It shows that distinct real guarded owners can overlap operations on one display in this candidate; it does not exercise two MCP server transports or prove a full user-facing admission flow. It also does not combine a new same-key hold with the precise r144 cleanup interleaving in one trial. No physical desktop, MAP01, queue-drain guarantee, or repeatability claim follows. The trial's `status: passed` means the observational protocol completed; it is not a claim of safety.
