- **Idea:** Test whether distinguishing observation noise, ontology/model uncertainty, dynamic state changes, and irreducible intent ambiguity selects better repeat/alternate/wait/UNKNOWN interventions than total uncertainty or a bounded decision-value policy.
- **Frozen scope:** Allocation `8641-T0-A01-20261009`; six authored profiles × 200 seeds = 4,800 policy rows; CPython 3.14.5 on macOS ARM64. Candidate had only observation-derived cues; oracle data was auditor-only. No model, GUI, user, live action, latency, or product behavior was tested.
- **Outcome:** The independent audit reconstructed all 4,800 rows with zero errors and rejected four construction corruption controls. Source classification was 5/6 (83.3%). DECOMPOSED was 88.9% correct at mean cost 0.258; TOTAL_UNCERTAINTY was 64.4% at 0.200; DECISION_VALUE was 85.5% at 0.158. The auditor-only ORACLE_INTERVENTION diagnostic was 86.4% at 0.133. All arms recorded zero hard-gate violations, but the preregistered requirement to reduce cost by at least 0.020 at matched correctness versus both practical policies failed. Do not relabel this as a positive efficiency result.

The exact ORACLE_INTERVENTION is auditor-only diagnostic. This matches the Issue's addendum distinction; do not compare the candidate against the exact oracle as if it were the deployed baseline.
